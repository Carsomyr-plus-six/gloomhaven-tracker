# Gloomhaven Campaign Tracker

A small website for tracking our Gloomhaven (first edition) campaign: the party, the city, scenarios, characters and a session log.

Live site: https://carsomyr-plus-six.github.io/gloomhaven-tracker/

It runs on GitHub Pages, so it works on phones and desktops without installing anything. On a wide screen, for running the campaign, it is one page. On a phone it is made for the players at the table, as four tabs along the bottom in the order of an evening, each keeping its place when you switch between them from the tab bar. Screens used once in a while (the Shop, enhancing, the session form, the scenarios to pick from, who's playing) open as sheets over the tab, closed with Close, Back or Escape:

- **Tonight**: the evening as a rail of four steps (the one to see to now lit), then what to sort out before playing (on a phone that isn't recording, just that phone's character's and what the whole table plays by, with a count of what waits for whoever records), then the scenario level as one line, opening who's playing and the difficulty, then the scenario. Tapping one under Ready to play picks it, and a card shows how to set it up: monster level, trap and hazardous terrain damage, gold a coin and bonus XP at tonight's level, whether a road event comes first, its treasure tiles, and each player's hand size and hit points. **Change** opens the others to pick from. Under it, the battle goal this phone's player kept, picked from the 24 cards and shown face down until tapped (and hidden again with another tap). The Scenario step goes to the scenario on this tab.
- **Play**: your character's sheet, starting with what's played with: tonight's scenario and its numbers, the ability cards (hand, discard pile and lost drawn as stacks, from the class's hand size, with plays, short and long rests, Undo, the rounds left at most, and when to rest or the character is exhausted), conditions (toggles for poison, wound, immobilize, disarm, stun, muddle, invisible and strengthen, with what each does, rulebook pp. 22–23), the items (a tap spends or consumes one, or marks a use circle; tapping again readies it; **Refresh** readies the spent ones), and the attack modifier deck, where Bless and Curse cards are counted. What's kept between scenarios (XP, gold, quest, checkmarks, perks, notes) follows. A tracker docked above the tabs keeps hit points (the class's maximum for the level), the XP on the dial, coins, the conditions that are on and the cards in hand under the thumb; a long rest's heal takes off Poison and Wound (and while poisoned heals nothing else); its reset (tapped twice) starts the next scenario. A tracker still holding numbers from another scenario or an earlier day says so on Tonight's scenario, with **Start fresh**, and once the evening it was used on is in `campaign.json` it clears itself.
- **Town**: once a scenario is under way, what it gave each character this phone tracked, to read out to whoever records (XP from the dial plus the bonus on a success, coins in gold at tonight's level, battle goal checkmarks, and the XP, gold, level and perks that leaves them with). Then what this phone's character has to see to in town: their gold and the Shop (opening on what they can buy), an ability card for a new level, perks to choose, the Sanctuary, and enhancing.
- **Campaign**: reputation, prosperity, the party, scenarios, sessions with the form to record one, event decks and the campaign record: the campaign at a glance; milestones with how far each has come (Envelopes A, B and X, the Town Records Book, and the class boxes and events reputation opens); achievements with what each opens or blocks among the scenarios found so far (lost and replaced ones struck through, with where); the 17 class boxes, closed ones shown by their icon alone; item designs as the Shop shows them; and the retired.

The first time a phone opens it, it asks which character you play. From then on it opens on Tonight, or on that character's sheet while a scenario is under way on it (a link to another part of the page still goes there). The Play tab carries the character's name and class icon, marks them "You" among the party, and goes back to their sheet whenever it's tapped; the Shop starts out shopping for them. The choice, the tracker, the battle goal and the pick are kept in that phone's browser only. One phone records the evening: the one holding a draft. On the others the event steps open the decks to read, the Shop has no **Buy** or **Sell**, and nothing records until **Record the evening on this phone** makes it the recorder. The form starts with the scenario picked on that phone, and offers to fill in the character's XP, coins and battle goal checkmarks from its tracker. Campaign data lives in this repository, so every change is a commit and the history of the campaign is the history of the repo.

## Recording a session

The top of the Overview walks through the evening in the order it runs: a city event, the town (shop prices, perks, the Sanctuary), a road event, then the scenario at tonight's level, each linking to where it's handled; the event steps open the form at an event row for that deck. As the draft fills in, each step says what's recorded and is ticked. **Record the evening** at the end of that row opens the form.

At the table, open **Sessions** and choose **Record a session** (or **Record the evening** on the Overview). Fill in:
- the date and who played
- each scenario attempted, with its level, the result, and for each character the XP on their dial, the coins they picked up and their battle goal checkmarks
- the treasures looted and who looted them, and who takes any item the scenario gives as a reward
- the events drawn, with the option
- characters retiring, with what their personal quest opens worked out from their quest card (a class box shown by its icon, so nothing is spoiled), and new characters joining
- items bought or sold, perks taken, and donations to the Sanctuary (10 gold each, once per player per visit)
- whether you opened Envelope B, once donations reach 100 gold

The page does the end-of-scenario sums itself:
- **XP and gold.** It adds the bonus XP for the scenario's level on a success, and turns coins into gold at that level's rate. Each character's row shows what it comes to. The level starts at tonight's level from the Overview, and the page says if it's outside easy to very hard for the party.
- **Rewards and treasures.** Scenario rewards and treasure gold and XP go to the characters, battle-goal checkmark rewards too; gold to share is split as entered in the form, and a choice of location (#13) is picked there. A looted item with no copy left gives its sell value instead (FAQ). Treasures count on a failure too, as their contents are gained when looted; a treasure's battle-goal checkmark goes to the looter; and an item the looter already owns is sold at once for half its cost, the copy going back to the shop (rulebook p. 27). The form offers the scenario's own treasure tiles to tap, and asks to check a number that isn't one of them.
- **Casual mode.** Any revealed scenario can be played in casual mode (the **Casual mode** tick, on by default for one already completed or blocked): XP, gold, loot, battle goals and personal quest progress count, but not its rewards or unlocks, and its record stays as it was (p. 34). No road event is drawn for it. A failure or casual replay keeps its results with the session (`results` in that session's scenario, with `"casual": true` for a replay).
- **Personal quests.** Nobody writes progress out: each step of a quest is set with a tap, on the sheet or in the form (see below), and the page writes it; once it's reached, the page says that character must retire before playing again (p. 48), and **Before you play** offers to record it. A quest that opens a box already open gives a random item design and a random side scenario instead (`questDraws` on the retired character, filled in once drawn).
- **Blessings.** A donation to the Sanctuary shows as "2 Bless" on the Tonight row and on that character's attack modifier deck, for their next scenario (p. 45). The page works this out from the donation's line in the log: the Bless waits for the donor's next scenario, that evening's unless the donation is marked as made back in town after it (`"after": true` on the line). A donation from before the logs began can be noted as `"blessNext": true` on the character instead; a flag the log contradicts is asked about.
- **Events.** An event's gold and XP go onto the sheets: "each" to everyone who played, and gold to share (or pay) split as you enter it under the event. Paying isn't a choice: if the party has the gold between them, it's lost and the outcome applies (p. 41), so **Paid?** starts from the party's gold; once paid, it comes off (gold the party paid split as entered, gold each paid from everyone, an item given up as a reminder). Every outcome of a choice that applies is resolved, "otherwise" only if none did. Battle-goal checkmarks events give or take go onto the sheets; a loss never costs a perk. Effects at the start of the next scenario (conditions, damage, discards) are listed in **Before you play**. A city event comes in town before the evening's first scenario, unless you pick **Back in town, after** the evening's scenario; on an evening in town only, it comes after the last scenario played (saved as `"after"`), and any effect it has at the start of the next scenario stays listed in **Before you play** until one is played. A road event counts as coming before the first scenario of the evening unless you pick another; none is drawn for a retry of the same scenario, one linked to the last, one linked to Gloomhaven (the scenario data's `eventType`), or casual mode (p. 41). A road event counts as coming before the first scenario of the evening unless you pick another. A loss can't take gold below 0 or XP below the start of a character's level. Where the outcome depends on something not recorded, the page says to add it by hand.
- **Prosperity.** When the city's prosperity level is above a character's level, **Before you play** says they may advance to it, and the form can record that (their XP goes up to the start of that level).
- **Level-ups.** Anyone who passes a level is told to add an ability card and take a perk. Until the next session, **Before you play** reminds them about the card.
- **New characters.** A new character starts at any level up to the prosperity level, with that level's XP and 15 gold per level plus 15. They play that evening's scenarios unless marked as sitting it out, and can loot, buy items and take perks in the same session. **Perks from retirements** adds one perk for each character their player has retired before (stored as `perkBonus`). A new campaign starts from an empty file: **Add the party's characters**, on the Overview or under Characters (Tonight or Play on a phone), opens the form for them, and the form names the party while it has no name.

It lists every change, with anything that looks wrong, before you save: an event that isn't in its deck, or a treasure already looted, is most likely a mistyped number; an item bought for a slot that's already full won't fit; a character can own only one of each item, and the shop has only so many copies. A bar at the top of the form says how many things there are to check.

The form starts with whoever is picked to play on the Overview's scenario level. In a scenario's number box, the scenarios the party can play are there to tap, and the scenario's name shows as you type.

Some things can be recorded straight from where you see them, each opening the form at the right row:
- **A perk to choose:** **Record it** in **Before you play** on the Overview, or on the character's sheet. Once picked, the perk stops being asked for while the draft waits to be saved.
- **A scenario you can play:** **Record it** in its details under Scenarios, at tonight's level.
- **Items:** in the Shop, pick who is shopping (this phone's character to start with). **Buy** shows on what they can afford and have room for, and **Sell** on what they carry; either puts it in the draft and stays in the Shop, the item then saying it's in the draft. Under each item, a mark per copy: empty while it's in the shop, its holder's class icon once carried (the shopper's own in verdigris, dashed while bought in the draft), with the words on hover and for screen readers. What the shopper can't afford, or the shop has none of, is faded with no **Buy**; a slot already full is said once, in the group's heading, as the item can still be carried. **Can buy** narrows the list to what they could buy. The Shop counts the draft: its purchases take gold, slots and copies, and its sales free them. On a phone, Buy and Sell show on the phone recording the evening only. In the form, a purchase, sale, perk or donation added on a phone starts with that phone's character.
- **An enhancement:** **Record it** on the enhancement calculator, for whoever is shopping or picked in the form; the gold comes off when it's saved.

A form opened and closed with nothing in it leaves no draft behind.

Only an evening of play goes in the session log: a perk taken, an item bought or a donation made between sessions goes in those characters' logs, and adds no session.

The site has nowhere to save, so it hands you the updated file:
- **Copy and edit on GitHub** (on the live site) copies it and opens `campaign.json` in GitHub's editor, which works on a phone too: select everything, paste, and commit.
- **Download campaign.json** or **Copy to clipboard**: put it in place of `campaign.json` in the repository and commit it.

Until then the draft stays in that browser, so a reload at the table loses nothing; **Discard draft** clears it. The file the page hands you carries the draft's id as `lastDraft`, so once it's in `campaign.json` the page notices on its next load and clears the draft itself, and nothing can be recorded twice.

Some things still need a hand edit afterwards, and the page says what to write: gold to share between the party, a choice of location, a random scenario or item design once drawn, and the option of an event recorded without one.

## Running it locally

Browsers won't load `campaign.json` from a page opened straight from disk, so run a small web server in this folder:

```
py -m http.server 8000
```

Then open http://127.0.0.1:8000/. Stop the server with Ctrl+C.

## Tests

The same checks run in Node and in a browser, and on GitHub with every push:

```
node tests/run.mjs
```

or, with the server above running, open http://127.0.0.1:8000/tests/. They check that:
- the reference data is pinned, every file to the same Secretariat commit;
- what the page works out from `campaign.json` and the sample (reputation and prosperity with every entry, achievements, item designs, classes, the event decks and each event's outcome, open questions, each scenario's status, who completed what, every character's sheet, the shop) matches `tests/golden/`, and comes out the same every time;
- each file is in the current layout: read and written back, it's unchanged byte for byte, and keeps nothing the page works out anyway;
- the campaign in the old layout (`tests/fixtures/`) works out the same and moves over to exactly the current file, as does a file in the layout before this one, every total kept;
- recording an evening with every part of the form (`tests/drafts/evening.json`) writes the file it should, the same every time: each change to a sheet a log line saying what it was for (a purchase's gold and item on one line), and each scenario's results in its session as played (the dial and the coins), what they gave worked out from its level;
- every personal quest card, for every character in `campaign.json` and the sample, counts from the record as `tests/golden/quests.json` says (with no progress written, so nothing ties a character to a card), as does each card with a count written by hand for a made-up character, joined as on the card and the other way, with the input each step gets (counted, a check box, or − and +); a tap writes the progress within what each step allows, with the card or from the record's steps alone; any count the page offers to record reads back and fits; every step that waits on another waits on the one before; who completed a scenario goes by its results and players, else the session; the form records only progress it can read;
- the milestones stand as they should;
- in a browser only (the test page, and on GitHub in headless Chrome), the page itself, opened in frames as a person would open it (`tests/smoke.js`): `campaign.json`, the sample and the old layout load without an error and draw every section and sheet, at phone width every tab opens, none wider than the screen; and the session form, on a copy of the campaign, takes every kind of row with each of its choices made and each quest shown, without an error.

When a change is meant to change what the page works out (a rule fixed, newer reference data), `node tests/run.mjs --update` rewrites the golden files from what it works out now; read their diff before committing it.

## The data format

`campaign.json` keeps what happened, each thing once (`"schemaVersion": 4`); everything else is worked out from it and the reference data. The evenings (**sessions**) are the one record of what was played; **scenarios** keep only what belongs to a scenario rather than an evening; each character keeps their sheet as their log began and a log of every change since. Every amount of XP, gold or checkmarks is in one place: what a scenario gave, in its results; anything else, a line in the character's log saying what it was for, with any item gained or lost and perk taken on the same line. If the file keeps something the page works out anyway, or something out of place, the Campaign record and Before you play say what to change. A file in the older layout (totals on the sheets, statuses on the scenarios) still reads, and the next file the form writes is in the new one: each completed scenario goes into the session that played it (one is made for a date no session has), a success there that the old file didn't count as a completion becomes a casual replay, and each sheet as it stood is kept, its start being what it had before the scenarios recorded. A file in the layout before this one (`"schemaVersion": 2`, which kept each evening's change to a sheet as one sum, and a play's XP with its bonus) moves over too, every total as it was: the sums go into the start, less what the scenarios gave, and the log keeps its items and perks.

It has these parts. Anything left out falls back to an empty value.

**party**: `name`, `location`, `achievements`, `notes`.

**city**: `start` (`{ "donations": 40, "returnedItems": ["Boots of Speed"] }`: gold given to the Sanctuary before the characters' logs began, and copies of items put back in the shop's supply before then; the rest is in the logs, and the Overview counts down to the 100 gold that opens Envelope B), `envelopeB` (`true` once you've opened Envelope B; from then on the page follows what it says about donations, as Gloomhaven Secretariat counts it), `globalAchievements`, `unlockedClasses` (a list of class names, written exactly as the class list has them), `itemDesigns` (items added to the shop permanently), `envelopeA` and `townRecords` (`true` once opened, which stops Before you play reminding you; it also reminds you of the class boxes at reputation 10 and −10, the events at 20 and −20, and the Drake events). Copies of items the shop doesn't otherwise sell, back in its supply, are worked out from the logs: every item `lost` on a line saying what it was for goes back (sold; looted and sold at once, a treasure line with the item both `gained` and `lost`; retired with, the retirement line listing everything handed back), less each copy bought back. A correction by hand, with no `for`, hands nothing back. A `returnedItems` list in a file from before (`"schemaVersion"` 3 or lower) joins `start`, less what the logs already account for; in a current file the page asks for it to be removed. A character written in as retired before the logs began hands back only what `start` lists.

Achievements, item designs and unlocked classes are mostly worked out, so these lists are usually empty. The page takes them from scenario rewards, event outcomes and treasures; the starting classes are always unlocked; a new City Rule or Artifact replaces the old one; and a lost party achievement is dropped (the Campaign record still shows both, struck through). List something here only when nothing else records it, such as a class unlocked by a retirement. The Overview says when an entry is already worked out.

Hand-listed achievements and item designs look like this. Only `name` is required. Write an achievement exactly as the scenario data names it, or give its `"id"`: scenario requirements are checked against it. One written any other way is a different achievement, which no scenario or event counts, and the Campaign record asks about it. Add `"count": 2` for an achievement gained more than once.

```json
{ "name": "Achievement name", "scenario": 2, "notes": "Optional" }
```

Reputation and prosperity aren't written down: the page adds them up from the rewards of completed scenarios, the outcomes of events, one prosperity checkmark per retired character, and any `adjustments`. When an event's outcome isn't known, the Overview shows the range it could be and asks about it. Open the tile to see where the number comes from.

**scenarios**: what belongs to a scenario rather than to an evening: the treasures looted there and notes. Whether it's completed, and when, comes from the sessions; which scenarios are unlocked, which are blocked and why, the rewards and the names come from the reference data. `treasures` lists the numbers of treasure tiles looted there, as text so leading zeros stay, and digits only, as printed on the tile. Numbers typed into the form are whole numbers or nothing: "12.5" or "12abc" isn't read as 12, and the form says so. A scenario with nothing to say needs no entry.

```json
{ "number": 2, "treasures": ["07"], "notes": "Played the same day as #1." }
```

The page looks up what each treasure gave: item designs go to the shop and unlocked scenarios open. A treasure that names an item gives one copy to whoever looted it, not to the shop (rulebook p. 14), so say who in the treasure (`"character"`) and list the item on their sheet; until someone is named or a sheet has it, the page asks. **Record it** on that question opens the form at **Settling the record**, where you pick who looted it and whether they still have it (if not, the copy goes back to the shop). A treasure can also be written out to say who looted it and, for a random side scenario or item design, what you drew. Who looted it is said once: a treasure recorded in the form is a line in the looter's log (`"for": "treasure"`, with what it gave), and the page reads the looter from there, so the scenario's treasure needs no `"character"`; it keeps one only for loot from before the logs began, or a tile that changed no sheet. If the scenario and a log name different characters, the Campaign record asks which is right. Until a random one is drawn, the Campaign record reminds you; `"drawn": false` says it never was and won't be, and stops the reminder.

```json
"treasures": [{ "number": "07", "character": "Craig", "drew": 52 }, { "number": "11", "drew": "Item name" }]
```

Say how a scenario was found only when the reference data can't know:

- A scenario unlocked some other way (a choice, a treasure, an event): `{ "number": 15, "unlockedBy": 13 }`, or `"unlockedVia": "a personal quest"` when no scenario opened it (the form writes this for a scenario a personal quest opens; never say whose, as only one quest opens each such scenario and this file is public).
- A scenario you've decided to treat as blocked: `{ "number": 5, "status": "blocked", "notes": "Why" }`

`name` is only for a scenario the reference data doesn't name.

**characters**: each sheet as its log began (`start`: `xp`, `gold`, `checkmarks`, `items`, `perks`, and `donated` or `enhanced` if any), and every change since in `log`, dated, a line for each thing that happened, `for` saying what: a scenario's `reward`, a `treasure`, an `event`, a `buy` or `sell`, a `donation`, an `enhancement` (with `what`), a `perk` taken, an `advance` to the prosperity level, a `retirement`. Each line has what it changed: `xp`, `gold` and `checkmarks` as amounts, items `gained` and `lost`, `perks` taken; so a purchase is one line, its gold and its item. A sheet's XP, gold and checkmarks are the start, the log, and what the character's scenarios gave (in the sessions' `results`); donations and enhancements are counted from their lines. The form writes the log, and a correction by hand is a line too, with no `for` (and a note in `notes` if it needs one); one the form wrote before lines said what they were for (an evening's items and perks together) reads the same way. A character written in by hand with plain totals (`xp`, `gold`, ...) and no `start` keeps those totals, and the next file the form writes has the start they imply. The page works out the level from the XP and the perks earned from the checkmarks. Under the perks, each sheet shows the attack modifier deck those perks make: the standard twenty cards, changed by each perk's wording, with any perk it can't read listed as written. A character retired in the form gets a retirement line in their log (`"for": "retirement"`, with the gold gone and the items handed back), and the page reads that they're retired, and when, from it; a retired character adds a prosperity checkmark and the class's retirement events. For one who retired before the logs began, set `retired` to `true` (and `retiredOn`, if known) on the character; if those disagree with a retirement line, the Campaign record asks. Set `inactive` to `true` for a character sitting out for a while: they keep their items (still out of the Shop) and can shop or take perks, but they aren't counted for tonight's scenario level, Before you play leaves them be, and they're hidden until someone taps **+ 1 inactive** (on the Characters section, the party list, the scenario level, the Shop or the form), which shows them everywhere after the others, quieter. If they turn up for a night, show them and pick them on the Overview's scenario level: they count for that night's level, and a new session starts with them playing. Marking them as playing in a recorded session makes them active again. Write `perks` word for word as the character sheet has them (the form does): a perk is the class's perk only when the wording is the same, and the page flags one that isn't (with the closest perk as a hint) or is taken more often than it has boxes, and, when one is due, shows the class's whole list in the sheet's order with the open perks quieter. A character can own any number of items, but equip only one head, body and legs item, two hands, and small items up to half their level, rounded up (p. 43): the sheet says when they own more than they can take into a scenario. Write `items` letter for letter as the item list spells them: the Shop counts those copies as out of stock (a retired character's items go back) and flags an item it doesn't know (with the spelling, if only capitals differ) or doesn't sell yet. Personal quests are secret, so `personalQuest` holds only the progress (like `"2/15"`, which also gets a progress bar), never the quest's name. A quest in steps is written one count per step, in the card's order: joined by `, then` when a step waits on the one before, like `"1/3, then 0/1"` (three of something, then one more thing), and by ` + ` when steps go side by side, like `"2/2 + 1/2"` (two of one thing and two of another). It gets a bar per step, and counts as fulfilled only once every step is. The page writes it from the steps as they're set; written by hand any other way, it can't be counted, and the Campaign record asks for it as counts. On the sheets and in the form the progress stays hidden until its player taps **Show**; once a quest is fulfilled it shows openly, as the character retires.

Which quest card a character holds is never in `campaign.json`, as the repository is public. Under a shown quest, its player picks the card, which that device alone remembers. That device then shows each step and counts what it can from the record (scenarios completed, by name, with a boss, or side scenarios; a scenario chain followed to its end; battle-goal checkmarks; gold; items held): those steps are set by the record, not by hand, and where it can't be sure (a scenario with no record of who played, donations from before the page kept them) they can be set up to what may have been missed. Steps it can't count (kills, exhaustion, regions) are set by hand: a check box for one thing (a demon, a region), − and + for a count; a step that waits on another stays as it is until that one is done. Each tap goes into the session being recorded, and the page stays where it is; if the record's progress doesn't fit the card (or joins the steps otherwise than the card does), it offers to record what does. In the form, **Personal quests that moved** shows the same steps for each character behind **Show**: from the card on a device that knows it, or else from the steps the record already has (each from 0 to its target, a step after `, then` waiting on the one before); with neither, the player picks the card there. A scenario counts for a character when a win of it names them, in its `results` or its `players`, or, with neither, in the `characters` of the session it was won in (casual mode counts too, p. 34; the same scenario never counts twice); one with no record of who played is listed as "1 more if they played #2". On that phone, **Before you play** also says when the quest opens a side scenario (the second step of five quests), and **Record it** puts it in the form. What a retiring character's quest opens comes from their card, never picked by hand: the class box (shown by its icon, as its name would be a spoiler until it's opened) or Envelope X (two quests open it). A quest done is no secret, so a device that doesn't know the card asks for it in the retirement row; until it's picked, nothing opens, and the form says so (a box picked by hand in a draft from before stays, marked as such, until the card is). The card stays in the row, to put right a wrong pick; two retirements whose cards open the same box that evening give the second random draws instead. Quest 519 counts only checkmarks from battle goals (the official FAQ), so checkmarks on the sheet beyond the recorded results show as possible, not counted. For the quests that count donations (525), enhancements (530) or other characters' retirements (532), the page counts `donated` and `enhanced` from the logs, and reads when each retired (above) and records `joined` on characters. A retiring character's gold is gone (p. 48); retirements are recorded after the evening's scenarios. A new or retiring character can name its `player`: a new character's extra perks then count that player's retired characters (p. 48), by the name exactly as written (the page says if a player is written two ways), and starting above level 1 asks for an ability card for each level, as well as the perks (p. 42).

```json
{
  "name": "Grunt", "class": "Brute",
  "start": { "xp": 102, "gold": 35, "checkmarks": 4, "items": ["Boots of Striding"], "perks": ["..."] },
  "log": [
    { "date": "2026-11-20", "for": "treasure", "treasure": "4", "gold": 15 },
    { "date": "2026-11-20", "for": "buy", "gold": -10, "gained": ["Iron Helmet"] }
  ],
  "personalQuest": "", "retired": false, "notes": ""
}
```

**sessions**: one entry per evening of play, and the one record of what was played. Each scenario played is in its session's `scenarios`, in the order played: `result` is `success` or `failure`, `level` is the scenario level it was played at, `"casual": true` marks a casual replay, and `results` says what each character did there, as played: `xp` on their dial, `coins` picked up, `checkmarks` from a battle goal made. What that gave them is worked out from the level: the bonus XP on a success, each coin's worth in gold, the checkmarks on a success. A play with no `level` (recorded before plays kept one) can't be worked out, so its `xp`, `gold` and `checkmarks` are what each took, as written. Where the results are lost, `players` can say who played it. Who was at the session is said once: the results name everyone there, so a session whose plays all have them needs no `characters`; it keeps one only where they can't say (no results kept, as for the early sessions). If both are there and name different people, the Campaign record asks. A scenario is completed by its first success that wasn't a casual replay. The sessions, in the order the file lists them, are the order things happened in, which decides reputation's limits, which City Rule replaced which, and where events fall: the form puts a new evening in by its date (one recorded late lands in its place; one with no date goes last), and the page says if the dates go backwards. A scenario marked completed with no session behind it still counts, and the page asks for it to be moved into its session. Personal quests that count scenarios use `results`, `players` and the session's `characters` to know who completed what. An optional `title` names a session: it shows under the date, or as the heading when there's no date. Who passed a level that evening is worked out from XP over time (the session's plays and the log lines dated with it; a new character joining above level 1 too), so **Before you play** can remind them to add an ability card. For a session whose plays have no `level` (written in before plays kept one), the page can't tell, and an optional `levelUps` list says it instead; one the XP contradicts is asked about.

```json
{
  "date": "2026-10-04",
  "scenarios": [{ "number": 2, "level": 1, "result": "success", "results": [{ "character": "Grunt", "xp": 9, "coins": 5, "checkmarks": 1 }] }],
  "notes": ""
}
```

**events**: city and road events drawn, in order, with the option chosen. The tracker works out which outcome you got, what it did to reputation and prosperity, and the decks. `scenario` is the scenario it was drawn before (optional); an event without one counts as happening just after the event listed before it. `date` is the evening it was drawn (the form adds it), so the session log shows it in that session; an event without one shows in the first session that played its scenario.

Many outcomes depend on something on the table. The tracker checks these itself where it can:

- **Who was there**: `characters` lists everyone in the party at the event, by character name. Left out, it comes from the session the `scenario` was played in. List the whole party: a class that isn't listed counts as not there.
- **Reputation**: checked against reputation as it stood then.
- **Paying**: add `"paid": true` or `"paid": false`.

The Overview asks when it can't tell. `"outcome": 1` (counting from 1) picks an outcome by hand, and `"card": "removed"` or `"returned"` overrides what happened to the card. If the option can't be known any more, `"unknown": true` stops the questions: reputation and prosperity keep the range the event could have given.

```json
{ "deck": "city", "number": 29, "option": "A", "characters": ["Craig", "Azorica"], "scenario": 8 }
```

**adjustments**: changes to reputation or prosperity that nothing else records, such as a correction. Optional `scenario` puts it just before that scenario; otherwise it counts at the end.

```json
"adjustments": [{ "reputation": -1, "prosperity": 0, "notes": "Why" }]
```

An event that gives a random item design takes `"drew": "Item name"` once you've drawn it.

**eventDecks**: cards added or removed some other way. Events added by other events, scenario rewards, unlocking a class or a retirement are already counted: `{ "added": [{ "deck": "road", "number": 31, "notes": "Why" }], "removed": [] }`.
